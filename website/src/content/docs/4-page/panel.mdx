A panel is a column of chrome standing on the paper: a header pinned to its top, a body that takes the room between, and a footer pinned to its bottom. An application's list beside its detail, an inspector, a drawer's contents are panels.

## Use it

```html
<ds-panel>
  <ds-row slot="header" fill>
    <ds-text literal strong>Projects</ds-text>
    <ds-button primary>New</ds-button>
  </ds-row>
  <ds-list>…</ds-list>
  <ds-row slot="footer"><ds-text tone="muted" literal>12 projects</ds-text></ds-row>
</ds-panel>
```

Content goes to the default slot; `slot="header"` and `slot="footer"` pin to the top and bottom. Give the panel its height from the page's layout (a [page](/4-page/page/)'s columns, `fill`). The body takes the room between them and may shrink to nothing, and when its content is taller it scrolls while the header and footer stay put. Rows inside it are one square high as everywhere; the panel adds no box or shadow of its own.

| Slot | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `header` | A row at the top: the panel's title and its actions. |
| Default | The body, which scrolls. |
| `footer` | A row at the bottom: a count, closing actions. |

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `header`, `body`, `footer` | The three areas, for a product's own styles. |

## Behaviour

The header and the footer keep their own height, and the body lays its content in a column. An empty header or footer takes no room. The panel draws on `--ds-paper` in the text colour and sets the shared type, so what stands in it needs no type of its own.

## Accessibility

A panel has no role. Where it is a region of its own, say so around it: put it in an `aside` or `section`, with a name. The reading order is the header, the body and the footer.

## API

The slots and parts above are its whole interface: it has no attributes, events or tokens of its own. On Apple platforms and Android it is `DS1Panel`.

Select `panel` in Sync 1's components export.
